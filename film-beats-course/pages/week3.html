Week 3

Grandmother as sample source

The synth is a sound source feeding the sampler this week. Sequencing comes later. This keeps the SP as the brain and avoids fighting two sequencers at once.

Session A — bass and drone

Step 1: Patch a sub bass

No patch cables needed. On the Grandmother:

  • OSC 1 — square or triangle, octave switch down.
  • OSC 2 — off, or mixed very low.
  • Mixer — OSC 1 up, OSC 2 and noise down.
  • Filter () — cutoff low, around 8–10 o'clock. Resonance near zero.
  • Envelope — fast attack, medium decay, sustain around halfway, short release.
  • Reverb — off.

Play a low note. You want weight without mud, and no audible filter whistle.

Step 2: Get it into the SP — the faster method

Sampling one note per pad across an octave works, but there's a better way: .

Sample one note (a low C), then SHIFT + pad 4 to enter chromatic mode, and pads 1–16 play that single sample across a chromatic scale verified. You get a playable instrument from one sample instead of twelve.

The trade-off: chromatic mode is pitch-shifting one recording, so notes far from the original get artefacts, and it occupies the pads for one sample at a time. If you want a bass you can play while other pads are firing, sample individual notes the original way — four or five notes across a fifth is enough for most basslines. Do the chromatic version this session; do multi-sampled notes only if you hit that limitation.

Step 3: Drone

  • Filter open, around 2 o'clock.
  • Envelope: full sustain, so the note holds as long as the key is down.

Session B — synth as glue

Step 1: Frequency placement

Film dialogue sits roughly 200 Hz–4 kHz, with most of its intelligibility between 1–3 kHz. If you put a mid-range synth line there, one of them loses. The synth's job is below (sub bass, 40–120 Hz) and above (airy , filtered noise). Not on top.

Step 2: Run film audio through the Grandmother

This is the technique that makes disparate sources sound like one record. The Grandmother's 1/4" external audio input feeds the mixer, then runs through the ladder filter, the spring reverb and the verified.

Connections:

  1. SP LINE OUT → Grandmother 1/4" instrument input.
  2. Grandmother 1/4" audio out → SP INPUT (or LINE IN).
Read this before you plug in. That's a feedback loop: the SP feeds the Grandmother, the Grandmother feeds the SP, and if the SP is monitoring its external input you can get runaway howl. Two mitigations:
  • Safe version: don't complete the loop. SP LINE OUT → Grandmother in, Grandmother out → Zoom H4. Record there, transfer the WAV by SD card, import to the SP. Slower, zero risk.
  • Live version: complete the loop but start with the Grandmother's VOLUME at minimum and raise it slowly. If you hear a rising tone building, you've gone too far. Keep [EXT SOURCE] monitoring off except when you're actually sampling.

One caution: the Grandmother's instrument input has no level control of its own verified. You set the drive amount from the SP's output level. Louder into the input = more saturation.

Also note: the envelope only opens the when a note is played.
